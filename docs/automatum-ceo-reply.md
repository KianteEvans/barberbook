**Subject:** Re: BOX Solutions — retainer outcomes, sequencing, and terms

Hi Aein,

Good challenge on the retainers — you're right, and the reason is structural. The Accelerator is the
only solution in the deck with a terms table. The three retainers share one slide where each tier is
three words in a pricing grid, so the deck reads as one product with a price list attached. We're
fixing that: each retainer gets its own page, and every rung carries what we commit, what we target,
and an included volume with a rate for exceeding it.

The line we're drawing on commit versus target is that **we commit only to what we control alone** —
a named owner at named hours, a cadence, artifacts issued, response times from a stated trigger — and
everything the customer's product, sales team or AWS determines sits in targets. That moves "close
deals" and "run a producing channel" out of the tier names entirely; those rungs get renamed to the
machinery we actually run, and the results they were reaching for move to the target line underneath.

One honest caveat on the numbers themselves. Most of them are staffing commitments, so we're pinning
them to our own capacity model before we publish any of them rather than writing figures we'd have to
walk back at month twelve. Every one is bracketed with an owner and a date in the working deck.

On the rest:

**Sequencing.** Two waves. Wave 1 is the Accelerator plus Alliances; wave 2 is Operations plus Partner
Development. That's the value chain in your own slide 10 split down the middle — get listed and run
the partnership, then operate the Marketplace and build the channel — and it pairs each retainer with
the work that precedes it. Ten ACE submissions per wave rather than twenty at once. Wave 2 opens on
wave 1's Milestone 1 approval.

**Standalone or line items.** Standalone, to four different buyers, one agreement per Solution ID, no
bundle SKU. Worth flagging that the sharper version of your question isn't the shared ladder — it's
that *getting listed* appears in three of the four: the Accelerator at $10,000, Alliances Essentials
and Operations Launch both at $2,500 a month. Four months of either retainer costs the same as the
Accelerator and advertises its outcome. We're proposing a stated boundary — the Accelerator produces
a listing, Alliances runs the partnership around one, Operations runs one that exists — and making a
published listing an entry condition on both retainer entry rungs. Want your view on that.

**Partners transacting.** Agreed, adopting it. It kills "unlimited roster" on the thesis slide and it
retires the middle rung's name, which currently promises to grow exactly the number you want dropped.
We'll report partners issuing offers, with Kiflo as the system of record so the count is auditable.

**Entry condition.** Recording it as confirmed by you. Two things to close it out: the stage and tier
exactly as they appear on your Partner Central profile with the Partner ID, and confirmation that
Automatum is lead partner of record on all four Solution IDs — the deck implies that and never says it.

**Commercial terms.** Agreed, and better on the call. We're building a partner economics page purely
as an agenda for it — flow of funds, split per line, Marketplace fee treatment, milestone cash and
credits, component cost recovery, renewal and exit. No numbers in it before we talk.

One dependency worth surfacing now, because it blocks us: we can't publish a single response-time
commitment on Operations or Alliances until we have matching internal turnarounds from your side —
offer issue, listing configuration submission, selling authorizations, and which Partner Central
instance ACE gets filed into. Related, and load-bearing for the whole Partner Development scoreboard:
can a third-party reseller issue offers against your listing while you're seller of record, and by
what mechanism?

End of week works. I'll have the revised pages ready to walk through.
