# BOX Solutions deck — revision spec

**Source deck:** `Automatum_x_OBP__BOX_Solutions_Internal.pdf` — 14 slides, internal, dated 2026-08-30.
**Trigger:** Aein's (Automatum CEO) reply to our solutions email.
**Status:** spec only. Whoever holds the `.pptx` executes it.

This document is written to be executed slide by slide. Every edit to an existing slide quotes the
current wording. Every new slide is drafted in full. Every unset number is a bracketed placeholder
with an owner in the register at the end — **nothing in this spec should reach a customer artifact
while it still contains a bracket.**

## 1. What Aein actually objected to

> "The Accelerator has a clear one — a published, transacting listing in 14 to 18 business days. For
> Alliances, Operations and Partner Development, what is the customer actually being promised at
> twelve months? The tier names use outcome language — build pipeline, close deals, run a producing
> channel — but I can't see the numbers sitting behind them."

**This is a layout problem, not a wording problem.** The deck contains exactly one contract-shaped
object: p7's Term / What-it-covers table (fee, payment, remediation, delivery). It belongs to the
Accelerator — a $10,000 one-off. The three retainers, which carry all the recurring revenue and all
the twelve-month risk, share a single slide where each rung is three words in a pricing grid.

| | Slides | Terms table | Per-rung detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketplace Compliance Accelerator | 1 (p7) to itself | Yes | n/a — fixed-fee project |
| Managed AWS Alliances | ⅓ of p8 | No | 3-word label |
| Managed Marketplace Operations | ⅓ of p8 | No | 3-word label |
| Managed Partner Development | ⅓ of p8 | No | 3-word label |

One retainer at the middle rung is $60,000 over twelve months — six times the Accelerator's fee.
The deck's information architecture tells the reader the project is a product and the retainers are
a price list.

Two aggravators sit underneath it:

1. **The deck's spine is price, not work.** p1 is titled *"Four services, one price ladder"*; p4's
   footer, p5, p8's grid, p10 and p12 all argue sameness. Only p7 argues content. A deck whose
   organising claim is "these four are the same underneath", which then supplies substance for one
   of them, hands the reader Aein's *"are these genuinely four solutions?"* question for free.
2. **The p7 table encodes bounded scope at a price** — 20 hours included, further blocks of 10 at
   $1,000. The retainers have no included-volume concept anywhere, so any commit column written for
   them is an uncapped promise at a fixed fee.

**The fix is parity of artifact.** Every solution gets the same five slots — commit, target, band,
customer inputs and clock start, evidence — with the retainers' slots filled per rung.

### Every ask, and where it is answered

Use this as the completion checklist — nothing may be left unmapped.

|---|---|

**The call agenda is slide 18's rows plus the ten questions in §7.**

### Slide 7 — What a rung is

The single slide that installs the fix as a portfolio rule, so the three retainer pages do not each
re-litigate it. Five slots:

1. **WE COMMIT** — only what OBP controls alone: named staffing and hours, cadence, artifacts issued,
   response times measured from a stated trigger. Every attendance-dependent item is written as OBP's
   act: *"scheduled, held and minuted; issued as a written review with the action log on the same SLA
   where the customer cannot attend."*
2. **WE TARGET** — results that depend on the customer's product, market or sales team, or on AWS.
3. **THE BAND** — every rung carries an included volume and an overflow rate, modelled on the
   Accelerator's own convention (20 hours included, further blocks of 10 at $1,000). *No uncapped
   commitment at a fixed fee.*
4. **WHAT THE CUSTOMER SUPPLIES** — named counterpart, data, access, approvals, and the input
   turnaround. OBP's clocks run from these being met.
5. **EVIDENCE AND REMEDY** — each commit names the artifact that proves it (dated pack, log, report).
   Commits carry `[remedy — service credit / make-good / termination right — to decide]`. Targets
   carry no fee adjustment, credit or refund, and are shown against actuals in the monthly report
   from month one.

**Footer:** *"A commitment OBP cannot honour alone is worse than none."*

## 6. Open decisions

Nothing below can be resolved from the deck. Each needs an owner and a date.

**Structural**

- **Parity** — approve that each retainer gets its own slide with its own commit / target / band
  table. This contradicts p7's current status as the only solution with a terms table; that
  asymmetry is the defect, so removing it is the point. Everything else depends on this.
- **Is the Accelerator a prerequisite** for Alliances Essentials and Operations Launch? (Recommended:
  yes, stated on both.)
- **Packaging** — confirm standalone sale, four buyers, four Solution IDs, no bundle SKU; decide
  multi-solution discount policy and the stacking rule.
- **The ladder** — confirm the identical $2,500 / $5,000 / $7,500 ladder survives. (Recommended: yes,
  defended as a convention.) If leadership judges the optics unsurvivable, settle it **before**
  commit/target work is drafted against these price points.
- **Rename cost** — is renaming rungs worth re-cutting up to sixteen customer artifacts under p14's
  mirror rule?

**Commercial**

- **Included bands and overflow rates** for every rung of all three retainers, each with a price for
  exceeding it. The house precedent is the Accelerator's 20 hours plus blocks of 10 at $1,000.
  *Without this, every commit column is an uncapped promise at a fixed fee.*
- **Remedy** on a missed commit — service credit, make-good, or termination right. *A commitment with
  no remedy is indistinguishable from a target, which collapses the whole split Aein asked for.*
- **Retainer payment terms** — billing cadence, monthly vs annual commitment, notice period,
  auto-renewal, mid-term step-up and step-down, treatment of unused band. **The Accelerator has a
  payment row; the retainers have none anywhere in the deck.**
- **OBP's own position before the call** — the opening split on each line and the walk-away below
  which the engagement is not worth delivering; whether OBP absorbs ASecureCloud and Kiflo cost;
  whether these terms cover only these four solutions or the whole relationship (which changes who
  is in the room).
- **Kiflo commercials** — pricing basis, seats per rung, edition per rung, pass-through above the
  allowance, and who owns partner agreements and the channel record at end of term.

**Delivery — the staffing model, the single largest input**

Named-owner hours per rung, whether a backup is funded at the bottom rung, desk coverage window and
time zone, and how many accounts one owner can hold. **Every response time and every cadence in every
commit column is a staffing commitment.** None is publishable until capacity-tested against the full
additive load at the top rung. *A response time honoured for two customers and missed for the sixth
is worse than no response time.*

Also: **concurrent capacity** for feasibility studies and milestone plans — the wave 1 pairing
assumes two. If the real answer is one, wave 1 splits and slide 20 changes.

**Metrics**

- Partner Development's headline definition: offer **issued**, offer **accepted**, or revenue
  recognised — and a rung name that matches. This is a commercial exposure decision, not a wording one.
- Whether target numbers are printed in the deck at all, or set per customer from their baseline at
  onboarding. **If the latter, the deck must still show the target metrics with units** — otherwise
  Aein's complaint is not answered.
- Whether partner types remain the tier axis (slide 4 says they do), which types sit at which rung,
  and what the upper rungs buy if the customer's market supports only one type.
- Confirm or retire *"nine services in three groups at every tier, with depth the only axis"* — and
  **name the nine services and three groups.** The deck asserts them and never names them.

**Governance**

Who may move an item from the target column to the commit column, and on what authority. Sales
pressure moves items one deal at a time; without a stated rule the structure erodes within a quarter
of first use.
